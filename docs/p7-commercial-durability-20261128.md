## Recommendation

Continue testing scoped delivery as the primary offer. Sell an evidenced business
outcome, reliable integration and a maintainable handoff; use reusable assets to
reduce our delivery effort. Assess each initial engagement without assuming any
renewal or expansion revenue. Offer ongoing work only where the client wants its
specific benefit. Do not fund an otherwise uneconomic build through hoped-for care
or restrict independent expansion to protect future revenue.

Inexpensive AI-assisted expansion could materially reduce demand for small paid
changes and expose generic assets to substitution. It can also reduce our own
costs and increase the number of useful workflows clients can afford. Neither
the net effect nor business failure follows from the premise alone. No observed
client cohort, delivery-cost breakdown or repeat-purchase record was found in
the reviewed materials; commercial durability remains unvalidated.

Preserve company-wide business use, expansion, integrations and independent
maintenance, including AI-assisted work by the client or another maintainer.
Distinguish those uses from standalone redistribution of reusable assets; do not
invent restrictions or final licence terms here. The accepted B intent and the
existing source-continuity follow-up remain authoritative.

## Where value and exposure sit

| Basis of value | Potential commercial benefit | Exposure and cost | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery expertise: problem selection, scope, acceptance and handoff | Client buys avoided mistakes and a working result; judgment may remain useful when code generation is cheap | Premium is unproven; capable clients or competitors can supply judgment too; discovery and review consume hours | A real process baseline, rejected alternatives, buyer's reason for purchasing and accepted result |
| Reliable integrations | Access, data mapping, exception handling, recovery and deployment can make implementation valuable beyond a demo | API changes, incidents and edge cases can erase the margin; connectors and AI may simplify the same work | Representative failure/recovery tests, deployment effort, defect/rework hours and impact of downtime |
| Optional ongoing services already contemplated in the offering | Client may prefer help with changes and upkeep despite being able to leave | No automatic renewal; response obligations and interruptions cost capacity; client edits may need diagnosis | Named recurring need, chosen maintainer, explicit scope and actual purchase/decline reason |
| Reusable templates/components embedded in delivery | Less repeated engineering, consistent tests and faster handoff can improve margins across projects | Packaging, compatibility, documentation and maintenance cost money; competitors can recreate generic functionality | Same component solves an independent client's need with measured net effort saved and no confidential reuse |
| Separately productized reusable assets | Potentially broader sales and lower incremental delivery effort | Distribution, onboarding, versions and support create costs; easy replication and DIY alternatives can compress willingness to pay | Repeated common demand, actual adoption/purchase evidence and support-adjusted economics before extraction |

These are hypotheses about mechanisms, not evidence of a defensible moat.
Company-wide use may remove opportunities to resell departmental variants to the
same client; count that honestly rather than narrowing rights. A growing client
may instead request genuinely new integration or reliability work. Both outcomes
are compatible with independent maintenance.

## Margin and repeat-demand sensitivity

For internal assessment, use net recognized project revenue after credits/refunds,
excluding collected tax. Deduct attributable delivery, review, testing, integration,
handoff and included correction labor; our AI/tool costs; direct third-party
expenses; and a stated risk provision. Then assess acquisition, shared overhead
and reusable-asset maintenance separately for business sustainability. Value
founder hours explicitly; unpaid time is not free. Avoid counting credited
discovery revenue twice or charging our delivery AI separately.

Illustration in normalized cost units only, not prices or measured outcomes:
revenue 100, coding labor 30, all other attributable costs 40 gives contribution
30. Halving coding labor gives 45 if review/rework stays unchanged. If competitive
pressure reduces revenue to 80, contribution becomes 25; another 10 units of
review reduces it to 15. Cheap generation can improve unit cost while competitive
pressure still reduces contribution. Record shared overhead separately; none of
these figures establishes profitability.

Use three scenarios in existing discovery/estimate notes:

- Client expands independently: assume zero later change/care revenue. Does the
  initial delivery cover its full effort and risk, and can the client operate it?
- Client asks us for later work: estimate each new scope and optional upkeep
  independently, including diagnosis of changed systems. Do not presume retention.
- Competition accelerates: compare the buyer's actual DIY/alternative offer with
  equivalent acceptance, integration, ownership/continuity and support scope.
  Recalculate contribution if obtainable fees fall and review costs persist.

Repeat revenue depends on eligible clients, actual recurring needs, their choice
to buy from us and contribution per engagement. More expansion opportunities
can coexist with a lower share captured by us. Track actual choices over a stated
observation period; an interview intention or care proposal is not a renewal.

## Evidence inside existing discovery

Use sections 2, 3 and 5 of the [discovery report](company-templates/discovery-report-template.md)
and internal estimate notes; no new service, database or evaluation platform is
needed. Keep seller costs and other clients' information out of the portable
client report.

1. Document a concrete current task: frequency, people, systems, exceptions,
   failure cost and realizable benefit. Identify who can authorize a purchase.
2. Ask how the client would extend it with AI or a replacement maintainer, which
   changes they can do unaided, and where they want help. Record an actual example,
   capability/access constraints and the reason for buying rather than assuming
   DIY inability or a willingness to pay for expertise.
3. Compare DIY/AI, existing tools and external delivery on the same done-state:
   data mapping, exception/recovery tests, deployment, documentation and handoff.
   Capture a concrete alternative and its exclusions; cheap prototype generation
   alone does not prove equivalence or inferiority.
4. Estimate and later reconcile hours by phase, reuse saved versus adaptation
   effort, included fixes and direct operating costs. Use comparable accepted
   tasks where available; do not authorize new inference trials through this note.
5. Record preferred maintenance arrangement, expected change frequency, optional
   help wanted and reasons to decline. At already-agreed follow-ups, distinguish
   actual paid work from intentions, self-maintained clients and lost opportunities.
6. Look across independent discoveries for a common reusable need and willingness
   to adopt it. Separate generic code from client secrets and bespoke requirements.
   Extraction is justified only if net saved delivery effort or supported sales
   evidence outweighs packaging and lifecycle costs.

Reconsider positioning if initial jobs require renewals to cover costs, review
consumes coding savings, buyers repeatedly prefer equivalent cheaper alternatives,
or recurring needs rarely convert. Strengthen the delivery hypothesis if accepted
outcomes earn purchases and margins hold without renewals. Strengthen the product
hypothesis only with repeated common demand and support-adjusted economics.
These are review signals, not invented numeric pass/fail thresholds.

## Owner choices and boundaries

No new material choice is required to complete this evaluation. The recommendation
does not change the accepted offering or allocate product-development resources.
If later evidence supports extraction, the owner must separately choose:

- Continue delivery-led reuse: preserves flexibility and limits packaging costs,
  but retains more bespoke effort and constrained delivery capacity.
- Prioritize a separately maintained product: can amortize common work across
  buyers, but commits capacity to distribution, support and compatibility amid
  replication risk.

Recommendation for now: delivery-led reuse until discovery supports the second
option. That future choice is conditional, not a pending instruction to build or
a new standing owner gate. The existing source-continuity gate owns the actual
unresolved rights decision; do not duplicate it.

No lock-in, mandatory subscription, price, new service, provider-spending grant,
client outreach, publication or contract approval follows from this evaluation.
Niche validation and accountant/legal review before client commitments remain
separate. Draft template alignment records existing intent only and settles no
source-access package or enforceability question.
