## Required presentation

- Offers overview: broad indicative ranges, clearly labelled estimates rather
  than agreed fixed fees, a nearby short scope/recurring-cost caveat and a link
  to the corresponding detail page.
- Project details: scoped examples, itemized first-year ownership budgets,
  assumptions, exclusions and fuller disclaimers. Separate required recurring
  costs, optional care and usage-sensitive estimates, including third-party
  costs paid directly by the client.
- First-year budget: discovery + build - applicable discovery credit + running
  costs for the stated 12-month window + applicable setup costs. If build is
  already the post-credit balance, do not subtract the credit again. State the
  window start, charged months, included periods, currency and tax basis;
  distinguish later renewals and commitments extending beyond the year. Avoid
  overlapping hosting/care charges. Our delivery AI costs stay inside our fee.
- Explain readiness dependencies neutrally: data cleanup, migration, access,
  process clarity and client participation can affect scope and estimates.
  Discovery reduces uncertainty without guaranteeing every hidden issue is found;
  document assumptions, client inputs and agreement of later scope changes.

## Alternatives and consequences

A (PL discovery floor / EN number-silent) preserves existing public approvals
and avoids unsupported build anchors, but gives weaker early budget guidance.
B (scope-specific floors) helps qualification, but minimums can mislead about
likely total cost. C (bands/examples plus first-year totals) supports orientation
and recurring-cost transparency, but needs maintained examples and can anchor
expectations or overwhelm readers. Progressive detail was the technical
recommendation; the owner now confirms it with the page placement above.
