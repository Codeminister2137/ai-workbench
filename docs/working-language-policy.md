# Working language: English work, Polish sources

Owner selected A on inbox `db097eef712c4a8586bc78acbbae86aa` on
2026-11-27; implemented 2026-11-28. No additional owner rationale was supplied.

Use English for analysis, internal records, summaries, decisions, and instructions.
Preserve Polish source material verbatim alongside English interpretations:
include relevant exact quotes and stable references to originals (message,
document/page, or file location). Retain the complete original in its approved
location; translations and summaries must never replace it. Keep private sources
private under existing access and retention rules.

Distinguish Polish quotation, English gloss, interpretation, and uncertainty.
Preserve context, conditions, exceptions, dates, negation, and amounts. If the
original is unavailable, label the interpretation unverified; never reconstruct
a quote. Source preservation makes nuance checkable, not automatically correct.

Keep key Polish terms with an English gloss on first use; maintain the glossary
below as recurring terms arise. For legal, tax, or accounting ambiguity, retain
the exact wording and flag it for the appropriate professional. Never turn an
uncertain translation into an established fact.

Polish correspondence, official forms, quotations, and Polish website copy stay
in Polish where their audience or purpose requires it. This internal convention
does not require rewriting existing records or translating every UI string.

For search, use original Polish terms and English glosses where useful. Future
translated search/index representations must reference originals. This policy
introduces no translation pipeline, embedding system, storage migration, or
external processing authorization.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

A was the technical recommendation and owner's choice: consistent English work
with auditable Polish evidence, at the cost of larger records and maintenance
discipline. B, language-of-source, avoids translation within a thread but splits
working language and complicates cross-language use. C, translating everything,
gives uniform English records but risks losing nuance and fossilizing errors.
