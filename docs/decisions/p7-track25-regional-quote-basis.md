## Accepted policy and owner rationale

Use a common scope/cost basis across PL/EN, dated currency conversion and itemized,
evidenced delivery differences. Calculate applicable tax separately. Language alone
establishes neither customer location nor tax treatment; no automatic EN, nationality
or foreign-customer multiplier is approved.

Foreign work adds tax, legal and accounting administration that the owner wants
compensated. Factor evidenced incremental costs into the scoped fee within the same
existing price ranges, without double counting. Applicable tax remains separate
from administration costs and margin. Examples to evidence per engagement include
specialist review, reporting, translation, currency collection and support windows;
none is an automatic charge for every foreign buyer.

The owner especially values remote-only work. Initially being below foreign market
prices is acceptable given weaker overseas marketing and fewer connections, unless
we heavily undersell ourselves. This is not a blanket discount: check delivery cost,
overhead, risk and margin before quoting. If viable delivery cannot fit the range,
revisit scope or seek an owner decision rather than silently increasing the range.
No numeric heavy-underselling threshold was supplied or is invented here.

A (recommended and selected) offers defensible, understandable parity but captures
less willingness-to-pay variation. B, value/segment quotes with explicit justification
and legal review, could capture high-value work but adds judgment, fairness/trust
risk and review burden. The owner chose A with the rationale above.
